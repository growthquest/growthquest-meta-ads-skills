# Lead Gen Static Ad Framework — Apply To Every Lead Gen Static We Make

**Why:** Pattern analysis of 16 winning/high-performing lead gen statics (May 2026 Van Media Inspo folder) revealed a tight, repeatable set of rules that every winner follows. Ignoring these rules produced the ads Van Media's previous batches looked like — branded, generic, ignorable. These rules are now the default.

**How to apply:** Use this checklist as the brief skeleton for any static lead gen ad — from concept selection through design QA — before any creative goes to a designer. Especially Van Media. Also applies to any other lead gen client (HVAC, dog trainers, financial advisors, agency offers, info products, B2B services, etc.). For ecom or brand awareness, this framework is NOT the default — use only the principles that transfer.

---

## The Macro Rule (non-negotiable)

**Lead gen statics must NOT look like ads.** They must impersonate native content the user already trusts and engages with: a tweet, an iOS Notes screenshot, a news headline, a TV chyron, a Calendly grid, a text message, a handwritten note, an apology letter, a friend's testimonial. Branded "ad-looking" creatives lose to disguise.

If a creative is recognizable as a Meta ad in the first 0.5 seconds, it's broken.

---

## The 7 Format Archetypes (pick disguise first, design second)

1. **Social-post mockup** — X/Twitter post (verified checkmark, real chirp font, real avatar styling). Hook = personal story or contrarian declaration. Body in tweet copy. CTA = "Tap to see how" at the bottom.
2. **Native-OS mockup** — iOS Notes, Calendly, Gmail, iMessage. Looks like a screenshot a friend forwarded. Black bg, yellow accents = Notes app. Cyan/teal grid = Calendly. Pixel-accurate.
3. **News headline / TV chyron** — Red "BREAKING" or "NEWS" banner. White headline strip. Real photo behind. Borrows newsroom authority. Use for big-number reveals.
4. **Plain-text billboard** — Pure black or pure red background, white text, no images, mixed font weights. Reads like a memo. Hook in caps, body in regular.
5. **Apology / open letter** — "OFFICIAL APOLOGY STATEMENT" or similar pattern interrupt header. Dense narrative paragraph copy. Brand logo only at the bottom. Best for warm/retargeting audiences.
6. **Handwritten / marker on paper** — Pure white background, handwritten font, black + one accent color (usually red for the punchline). Real hand or fragment as visual. Anti-corporate, feels personal.
7. **Branded testimonial / niche callout** — Only archetype where brand can show up at the top. Pairs a sharp identity hook ("STOP BEGGING for a $5K raise") with an embedded testimonial card (photo + 5 stars + quote + Verified Student badge) and a soft CTA pill.

Picking the disguise is step 1 of every brief. Never start with "what should the ad look like." Always start with "which of the 7 is this concept?"

## Hook Rules (top 25% of canvas)

Every hook must do one or two of these in the first 3 seconds:

| Hook type | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Specific number + timeframe | "$10K/month in 90 days" / "209 leads in 9 minutes" / "10–30 appointments each month" | always include unit + deadline |
| Identity callout | "For Financial Advisors, Annuity Agents or RIAs" / "We're looking for 20 committed dog trainers" | name the EXACT persona |
| Contrarian declaration | "Why Boosting Posts Doesn't Work" / "STOP BEGGING for a $5K raise" | flip the assumption |
| Personal story flex | "I'm 29. Last month I made $500K..." | first-person, specific stakes |
| Direct guarantee | "I guarantee... or I'll work for free" | absolute language |
| News framing | "BREAKING:" / "NEWS:" | borrowed authority |
| Curiosity reveal | "I've decided to show you my entire $10m/year business..." | tease the mechanism |

Stack two of these whenever possible. "Specific number + identity" is the most reliable combo.

**11 of 16 winners used ALL-CAPS in the hook.** The exceptions were tweet-format (sentence case = realism) or heavy-weight title case.

---

## Color Rules (max 3 colors, ideally 2)

| Pattern | When to use |
|---|---|
| Pure black bg + white text | Default. Tweets, billboards, OS mockups. Safest, highest contrast. |
| Pure red bg + white text | Maximum urgency. Guarantee ads. Stop-sign effect. |
| Pure white bg + black/red text | Handwritten or "real document" feel. |
| One saturated accent | Yellow underlines, neon green CTAs, red emphasis word. Used for ONE word, not whole sentences. |
| Red + Black + White | Newsroom palette. News mockups, HVAC-style, testimonials with red outlines. |
| Cyan/teal gradient + white | "Tech / digital product" cue. AI offers, SaaS, dashboards. |

Hard rule: never more than 3 colors. Extreme contrast between text and background, always.

---

## Branding Rule (this is the biggest mindset shift)

**Default = no logo, no brand mark, no brand colors.** 10 of 16 winners had zero brand presence.

The 6 that DID show brand placed it subtly: small top-center logo (Unleashed), tucked into a footer with Trustpilot (AI Partner), or inside a testimonial card. Brand never competes with the message.

**The lesson for Van Media:** save the logo for the landing page. The static's job is to look like content, not a Van Media ad. If the static reads as "branded," it's losing to the ones that don't.

This is the opposite of brand-awareness ad design. Direct-response = hide the brand, lead with the message.

---

## Typography Rules

Three-tier hierarchy every winner uses:

1. **Hook tier** — 2x–4x body size. Black or Extra Bold weight. All-caps (most cases).
2. **Body tier** — Regular or Medium. Same font family. Carries the proof.
3. **Emphasis tier** — Specific words inside the body are bolded / colored / underlined / boxed. Never whole sentences.

Font choices:
- Sans-serif everywhere except handwritten archetype
- Tweet mockups MUST use the real X typeface (Chirp lookalike), real verified checkmark, real avatar styling. Pixel realism is the trick.
- All-caps for hooks is the default; sentence case only when the disguise demands it
- Italics rare; one accent line max

---

## Value Prop — always 3 components stacked

Every value prop must have:

1. **Outcome** — what they get (number + unit)
2. **Timeframe** — when (90 days, 9 minutes, each month)
3. **Risk reversal** — why it's safe to believe (guarantee, or we work for free, or your money back, etc.)

All three in the first 5 seconds. Risk reversal is the difference between "wishful" and "trusted."

**Always include the negatives.** "No courses. No gurus." / "No chasing leads required" / "No networking. No dinner seminars." Telling the reader what they're NOT signing up for defuses skepticism. This was the most underused move in Van Media's prior batches.

---

## Copy Structure (story/tweet/apology formats — 120–250 words)

1. Hook (numbers + outcome + timeframe)
2. Why am I telling you this (credibility line, often a stat)
3. What it is (offer in one sentence)
4. Social proof drop (named student, named result, e.g. "Lachlan left his 9–5 in 3 months")
5. What's NOT required (negatives)
6. Risk reversal (guarantee)
7. CTA (low-friction, demoted)

For typography/billboard ads, compress to 25–50 words. **Avoid mid-length copy (60–110 words) — it dies in this set.** Either short-form billboard or full-story tweet/apology.

---

## CTA Rule — demote the CTA

The CTA is NEVER the loudest element. Reader is hooked first, then drops down to a soft CTA almost as an afterthought.

Acceptable CTA styles:
- Buried at end of body: "Tap to see how" / "Learn how below 👇" / "Learn More (Free Training) 👇"
- Soft button (rounded, gradient gold or red): "Launch Your Agency" / "Schedule Your Free Demo" / "Click Here to Learn More"
- Implicit (no button at all) for text-only, news, and handwritten formats

Emoji pointers (👇 👉) for non-aggressive directional attention.

**Never use a giant, screaming CTA button as the visual centerpiece.** That's brand-ad design, not DR.

---

## Psychology Stack (what makes the format work under the hood)

- **Pattern interrupt** — apology, BREAKING, handwritten, plain text-only. Disrupt feed rhythm.
- **Authority transfer** — news framing, verified checkmarks, Trustpilot stars, "Verified Student" badges.
- **Specificity bias** — "209 leads in 9 minutes" beats "lots of leads fast." Always one weirdly specific number.
- **Identity matching** — hook calls out the exact persona for self-selection in the first second.
- **Loss/risk reversal** — "Or we work for free." Shifts perceived risk.
- **Negative anchoring** — list the painful alternative.
- **Curiosity gap** — tease the mechanism, force the click to find the answer.
- **Disguise** — the umbrella mechanism. Looks like content, not like an ad.

---

## Brief / QA Checklist (run before any lead gen static ships)

Before approving a lead gen static, confirm:

- [ ] Which of the 7 archetypes is this?
- [ ] Hook uses specific number + timeframe OR identity callout (ideally both)?
- [ ] Max 3 colors? Extreme contrast?
- [ ] Brand demoted or absent?
- [ ] Hook in caps + black weight? Body in regular?
- [ ] Value prop = outcome + timeframe + risk reversal?
- [ ] At least one weirdly specific number?
- [ ] Negatives included ("No X. No Y. No Z.")?
- [ ] CTA demoted (not the loudest element)?
- [ ] Copy length is short-form (25–50 words) OR full-story (120–250)? Not mid-length?
- [ ] Could this be mistaken for organic content in the first 0.5 seconds?

If 2+ items fail, the creative is not ready.
